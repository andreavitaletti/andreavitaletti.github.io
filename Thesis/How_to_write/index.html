    Warning

    This structure is a starting point rather than a rigid prescription. For a thesis that develops and evaluates a technical system, the separate chapters on technical components and experimental methodology are particularly useful. For a primarily theoretical thesis, they can be adapted accordingly. REMEBER: It is your thesis, you probably know better than others what are the main messages and how to deliver them.

    Thesis Title

    Author: Author Name

    Abstract

    Aim: Summarize the problem, the approach, the main results, and the contributions of the thesis in a concise, self-contained text. The abstract is usually finalized after completing the main body.

    1. Introduction

    1.1 Problem Statement

    Aim: Define the problem precisely, explain why it matters, and identify the limitations of existing practice that motivate the work. Focus on the problem rather than presenting the proposed solution in detail.

    1.2 Motivation and Scope

    Aim: Explain why the problem is worth investigating and establish the boundaries of the thesis. Clarify what is in scope, what is out of scope, and which assumptions guide the work.

    1.3 Research Objectives and Contributions

    Aim: Summarize what the thesis seeks to achieve and identify its concrete contributions, distinguishing intended objectives from the results actually delivered.

    • Objective 1: ...
    • Objective 2: ...
    • Contribution 1: ...
    • Contribution 2: ...

    1.4 Thesis Structure

    Aim: Provide a brief roadmap of the chapters and explain how they fit together.

    2. State of the Art

    Chapter aim: Critically review the literature and existing solutions relevant to the problem. Organize the discussion around approaches, concepts, and limitations rather than summarizing papers one by one. Conclude by identifying the research gap.

    2.1 Background and Concepts

    Aim: Introduce the terminology, concepts, theories, and technical foundations needed to understand the problem and the subsequent review.

    2.2 Existing Approaches

    Aim: Describe and compare the most relevant existing methods, systems, and research directions. Explain their main ideas and the contexts in which they are useful.

    2.3 Comparison and Limitations

    Aim: Compare existing approaches using explicit criteria. Discuss their strengths, weaknesses, assumptions, and limitations in relation to the problem.

    2.4 Research Gap

    Aim: State what remains unresolved in the literature and why it matters. The gap must follow from the reviewed evidence and motivate the research questions.

    3. Research Questions and Methodology

    Chapter aim: Translate the research gap into answerable questions and explain the strategy used to investigate them. Make the connection between the state of the art, the questions, and the planned evidence explicit.

    3.1 Research Questions

    Aim: Formulate a small set of precise questions. Each question should address an aspect of the research gap and be answerable using the methods and evidence presented in the thesis.

    • RQ1: ...
    • RQ2: ...
    • RQ3: ...

    3.2 Rationale in View of the State of the Art

    Aim: Explain why each research question is necessary given the strengths and limitations of prior work. Clarify what is already known, what remains uncertain, and what answering the question will contribute.

    3.3 Research Strategy and Evaluation Plan

    Aim: Explain how each research question will be investigated and what evidence will be used to answer it. Introduce hypotheses, evaluation criteria, datasets, and experiments at a high level.

    Research question Method or experiment Evidence needed
    RQ1 ... ...
    RQ2 ... ...
    RQ3 ... ...

    4. System Design and Technical Components

    Chapter aim: Present the solution, system, model, or technical method developed in the thesis. Explain its design and operation clearly enough for readers to understand how it addresses the problem and how it can be evaluated.

    4.1 Requirements and Design Goals

    Aim: Derive functional and non-functional requirements from the problem statement and research questions. Explain the criteria guiding the design decisions.

    4.2 Architecture and Overview

    Aim: Present the overall architecture and the relationships among its components. Use a diagram where appropriate and explain the flow of data or control.

    4.3 Technical Components

    Aim: Describe each major component, algorithm, model, or module, focusing on the design choices relevant to the research questions. Justify important decisions and discuss alternatives where appropriate.

    4.3.1 Component A

    Aim: Explain the component's purpose, inputs, outputs, operation, and design rationale.

    4.3.2 Component B

    Aim: Explain the component's purpose, inputs, outputs, operation, and design rationale.

    4.4 Implementation

    Aim: Document the implementation details necessary to understand the realization of the design, including technologies, configurations, and non-obvious decisions. Move lengthy code listings to an appendix or repository.

    4.5 Chapter Summary

    Aim: Summarize how the design addresses the problem and identify the aspects that will be evaluated experimentally.

    5. Experimental Methodology

    Chapter aim: Explain how the proposed approach is evaluated. Describe the experiments transparently and in enough detail to support fair comparisons and reproducibility. Keep the procedure separate from the results.

    5.1 Experimental Questions and Hypotheses

    Aim: Translate the research questions into observable outcomes. Where appropriate, formulate hypotheses and specify what evidence would support or contradict them.

    5.2 Data, Materials, and Test Environment

    Aim: Describe the datasets, participants, hardware, software, tools, or other materials used. Report relevant selection criteria, preprocessing, versions, and conditions.

    5.3 Baselines and Comparison Methods

    Aim: Identify the baselines or alternative methods used for comparison and justify their suitability. Ensure that comparisons are meaningful and use equivalent conditions where possible.

    5.4 Metrics and Evaluation Criteria

    Aim: Define how performance or outcomes are measured. Explain why each metric is appropriate, how it is computed, and how the measurements will be interpreted.

    5.5 Experimental Procedure

    Aim: Describe the steps of each experiment in sufficient detail for another researcher to reproduce it. Identify the variables changed, the variables controlled, and the data collected.

    5.6 Threats to Validity and Reproducibility

    Aim: Identify factors that could affect the credibility or generalizability of the findings, including sampling, measurement, confounding variables, and implementation choices. Explain mitigation measures where possible.

    6. Results

    Chapter aim: Report what the experiments found using figures, tables, and appropriate quantitative or qualitative evidence. Present the findings clearly and neutrally; reserve their broader interpretation for the discussion.

    6.1 Results for RQ1

    Aim: Present the observations and measurements that answer RQ1, referring to the relevant metrics, tables, and figures.

    6.2 Results for RQ2

    Aim: Present the observations and measurements that answer RQ2.

    6.3 Results for RQ3

    Aim: Present the observations and measurements that answer RQ3, if applicable.

    6.4 Additional Findings

    Aim: Report relevant findings not directly anticipated by the research questions. Distinguish exploratory findings from planned analyses.

    7. Discussion

    Chapter aim: Interpret the results in relation to the research questions, the original problem, and the state of the art. Discuss both positive and negative findings rather than simply repeating the Results chapter.

    7.1 Answers to the Research Questions

    Aim: Provide a direct, evidence-based answer to each research question. Explain which results support the answer and where uncertainty remains.

    7.2 Comparison with the State of the Art

    Aim: Explain how the findings compare with previous work. Clarify whether they confirm, extend, challenge, or differ from earlier results, and discuss plausible reasons.

    7.3 Implications

    Aim: Discuss the implications of the findings for research, design, implementation, or practice. Distinguish conclusions supported by evidence from possible future applications.

    7.4 Limitations

    Aim: Explain the limitations of the approach and evaluation and how they affect the conclusions that can reasonably be drawn.

    8. Conclusion and Future Work

    8.1 Conclusion

    Aim: Return to the original problem and summarize the main contributions and findings. State what the thesis has established without introducing new results or making claims stronger than the evidence supports.

    8.2 Future Work

    Aim: Identify valuable next steps arising from the findings and limitations. Explain how each direction could extend, validate, or improve the work.

    Bibliography

    Aim: Provide complete and consistent references for all sources cited in the thesis, following the required citation style. Every cited work should appear in the bibliography, and every listed reference should be cited in the text unless the applicable style allows otherwise.

    • Author, A. (Year). Title of the work. Publisher or venue.
    • ...

    Appendices

    Aim: Place supporting material that is useful for verification or reuse but would interrupt the main narrative, such as extended tables, questionnaires, supplementary proofs, configuration files, or detailed experimental instructions.
